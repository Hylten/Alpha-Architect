Origination is a conversion problem before it is a technology problem. The agentic stack captures millions of signals daily, but only a fraction convert to targets, and only a fraction of those convert to conversations. The firms that measure the pipeline between capture and term sheet close the gaps that their competitors leave invisible.

The traditional model cannot measure this chain. Manual screening produces a deal count, not a funnel. The agentic model produces both. Each stage has a conversion rate, each conversion rate has a lag, and each lag is a cost line that compounds across the fund cycle. Per CT Acquisitions' 2026 sourcing playbook, five channels carry the majority of lower-middle-market deal flow, and the gap between the first signal and the first conversation is the largest source of lost deals.

## The the stages of the Signal-to-Deal Funnel

The agentic origination pipeline runs through its stages: capture, indexing, scoring, outreach, and conversion. Each stage filters the input and adds structure to the output.

The capture stage indexes raw market signals, company registries, press releases, financing filings, conference rosters, and intermediary teasers. Per Accenture's 2026 analysis of agentic AI in private equity, autonomous scanning of these sources surfaces targets before the market reacts. The capture rate at this stage is high by design: the system indexes everything in the sector, not just what a human would flag.

The indexing stage converts raw text into structured data. Names, ownership chains, financing events, and hiring patterns are extracted and normalized. This stage determines how much of the captured signal survives into the scoring model. Industry estimates put the indexing yield at roughly 80 to 90 percent, with the loss coming from unstructured formats that resist extraction.

The scoring stage ranks targets by sale intent probability. The model weighs ownership succession profiles, financing registration gaps, and operational stress patterns. The conversion rate from indexed signal to ranked target falls between five and fifteen percent, per our assessment of proprietary deal platforms operating in the lower-middle market.

The outreach stage initiates contact with scored targets. GTM-engineered messaging references the company's actual financing history and sector position. The response rate at this stage varies by channel, and the five-channel aggregate yields a response in two to eight percent of cases according to industry benchmarks.

The conversion stage moves a respondent to a term sheet. This is where the pipeline thins most aggressively. Of the targets that respond to outreach, roughly one in ten to one in five proceed to a signed term sheet, reflecting the friction between initial interest and serious engagement. The full-funnel conversion rate, from captured signal to signed term sheet, lands near one-tenth of one percent, which means the agentic stack's value is not in any single stage but in operating the funnel at a scale where one-tenth of one percent still produces dozens of proprietary opportunities per quarter.

## The Conversion Rate That Defines the Edge

The edge in agentic origination is not the model. The edge is the volume at which the funnel operates. A human team captures a few hundred signals per week and converts one or two. An agentic layer captures every signal in the sector and converts at the same one-tenth of one percent rate, but the absolute volume makes the difference.

Per the FSb's May 2026 report on private credit vulnerabilities, the origination-centric definition of private credit places the infrastructure layer as the primary differentiator between operators. The firms that own the capture infrastructure do not compete on a deal-by-deal basis. The firms compete on who can run the funnel fastest, widest, and with the least friction.

The conversion rate at each stage is a lever. Improving the indexing yield from 80 to 90 percent doubles the ranked target pool without touching the capture layer. Improving the scoring precision from five percent to ten percent doubles the response-eligible targets. Each percentage point of improvement at an upstream stage propagates through every downstream stage.

## The Feedback Loop That Compounds the Funnel

The agentic stack improves its own conversion rates through feedback. Every completed deal teaches the scoring model which signals predicted the outcome. Every response teaches the outreach engine which message opened the door. The improvement is the moat.

Per Ideas2It's 2026 guide to agentic AI in private equity, the agent monitors company databases, filings, and market signals, then scores opportunities against the accumulated history of previous outcomes. The scoring model weights the signals that correlated with successful term sheets and deprecates the signals that did not.

The compounding is visible in the data. A platform that has processed 500 completed deals and 12,000 outreach responses has a signal library that no competitor can license. The competitor can buy the same data feed and run the same model. The competitor cannot replicate ten years of structured market history.

The velocity dimension matters as much as the conversion dimension. A target that converts in seven days carries a different value proposition than a target that converts in forty-two days. The lag between capture and conversion is the cost of capital drag on the deal, and the firms that measure and minimize that lag close before the market wakes up.

## The Data Boundary: Where Automation Ends

Not every signal converts to a deal, and not every deal should be automated. The data boundary is where the target's financial information becomes too sparse for the model to score accurately. Small private companies file minimal disclosures, and the scoring confidence drops below the outreach threshold.

Industry estimates suggest that roughly 30 to 40 percent of the indexed targets fall outside the data boundary, where human judgment must supplement the model. The fractional deal principal model handles this boundary explicitly: the infrastructure identifies and qualifies, and the principal leads the conversation from first contact to signed term sheet. The combination is the off-market machine.

The boundary also moves with regulation. Per the FSb's private credit vulnerabilities report, markets with digitized registries and electronic collateral administration cross the data boundary faster than markets with paper perfection. The agentic stack performs best where the data is cleanest, and the gap between data-rich and data-poor markets is where the referral channels earn their keep.

## The Principal's Metric

The family office principal asks one question: what arrives in my pipeline that no one else sees. The agentic stack answers with coverage, speed, and proprietary construction. But the principal's real metric is the deal that closes without a public auction, at a price the market has not yet discovered.

The conversion pipeline measures that outcome. Capture rate, indexing yield, scoring precision, response rate, and close rate each pass through the funnel. The firms that optimize each stage own the off-market supply. The firms that treat origination as a relationship function, not a conversion function, watch the edge migrate to those who built the funnel.

Per ABF Journal coverage of the origination infrastructure shift, banks and private credit managers are building data layers that neither could construct alone. Overland Capital deployed more than 7 billion USD, roughly 77 billion SEK, in transactions since launch, and the reporting notes that the origination infrastructure, not the capital stack, is the primary reason for the sourcing advantage.

The systems mandate applies across the stack. Signal capture, reasoning, construction, and execution must operate as one pipeline with measurable conversion at each stage. A gap anywhere in the chain converts into lost deals, and the firm that measures the chain closes the gaps.

## Summary

The agentic origination funnel converts millions of daily signals into proprietary deals through the stages: capture, indexing, scoring, outreach, and conversion. Each stage has a measurable rate and a measurable lag, and the improvement at any upstream stage propagates downstream. The full-funnel conversion rate lands near one-tenth of one percent, which means the edge is volume, not precision. Firms that measure each stage, minimize the lag, and own the accumulated feedback library control the off-market supply. The principal who owns the funnel closes before the market wakes up.
