The Origination Layer: Agentic Screening and the New Economics of Proprietary Deal Flow

August 10, 2026 5 min read • Jonas Hyltén

A family office principal reviews the same teaser that forty other buyers received. The auction is already decided. The seller's advisor ran a controlled process, the data room opened to a fixed list, and the price discovery reflected the buyers who were invited, not the buyers who were right. The deal was never the problem. The access was.

Proprietary flow is an information problem before it is a relationship problem. The firms that construct deals before the market reacts do so through a technical layer: continuous data capture, automated screening and disciplined scoring. Per Accenture's December 2025 analysis of agentic AI in private equity, intelligent agents now scan filings, sentiment and sector chatter autonomously and surface targets before the market reacts. That capability is the origination layer, and it changes the cost structure of finding a deal.

The Data Capture Problem

Manual sourcing starts with a list. The list ages the moment it is built, because company registries, filings and ownership changes update continuously while the list sits static. The technical response is capture at scale: structured feeds from corporate registries, financial statements, court records and news sources, ingested on a schedule measured in hours rather than quarters.

The commercial data providers already sell this raw material. Per WorkWise Solutions' 2026 comparison of AI deal sourcing tools, Grata, SourceScrub and Cyndx dominate discovery, while Affinity and DealCloud handle relationship intelligence. The tools are inputs, not answers. The edge comes from what the firm does with the feed: which signals it extracts, how it scores them against a thesis, and how fast it converts a score into a conversation.

Scoring Against a Thesis

Screening is a scoring problem. The firm defines the thesis as machine-readable criteria: revenue band, ownership structure, age of the register, litigation history, financing events, hiring patterns. Each data point becomes a filter, and the filters combine into a score. The score does not replace judgment. It ranks the candidates so judgment lands where it matters.

The Navatar Group's 2026 analysis of thematic deal sourcing describes the step change as overlaying AI on an existing data model, moving from periodic manual list building to continuous scoring. The distinction matters. A quarterly list captures the market as it was. A scored feed captures the market as it moves. For a mid-market mandate with a defined thesis, the difference is the difference between finding the target in its quiet phase and finding it after the teaser went out.

The Conversion Step

Screening produces candidates. Conversion produces conversations, and conversion is where most systems fail. The scored target needs a first touch that matches the thesis, the sector and the timing. Technical firms run this step as an outreach pipeline: personalised sequences generated from the same data that produced the score, sent through the channels the target actually reads.

Per Accenture's survey of 250 PE professionals, leadership confidence in customer segmentation and targeting rose from 3.5 in 2023 to 3.9 in 2025 on a five-point scale. The direction is consistent with the market data. Accenture reports that AI and machine-learning PE deal value more than tripled from 41.7 billion USD in 2023 to 140.5 billion USD in 2024, roughly 1.55 trillion SEK at current rates, and grew from 3 percent to 8 percent of total deal value. The capital is following the capability.

Governed Autonomy

The objection to agentic screening is control. The answer is governed autonomy. Accenture's framework ties each agent to measurable outcomes and defines human-loop thresholds: the system screens, scores and drafts, and the principal decides on the shortlist. The machine expands the funnel. The human holds the gate. That division of labour is what makes the layer safe enough to run continuously.

The governance requirement is practical. Every score needs an audit trail back to the source data, because a wrong score from a stale feed is worse than no score. Every outreach sequence needs a kill switch, because a target approached at the wrong time is a target lost. The firms that treat the origination layer as infrastructure, with logs, thresholds and review cycles, scale it. The firms that treat it as a novelty run it once and shelve it.

The Cost Curve

The economics are the point. A manual sourcing professional produces a bounded number of conversations per month, and each conversation costs the same regardless of quality. An agentic layer produces a scored universe at near-zero marginal cost per candidate, and the human hours concentrate on the top of the funnel where they generate conversations. The fixed cost moves from the person to the pipeline, and the pipeline scales with data, not headcount.

The limitation deserves stating. No layer replaces underwriting, and no feed replaces judgment. The agentic system finds and ranks. The principal still reads the company, tests the thesis and sits in the seller's office. What the layer removes is the lottery: the dependence on which teasers happened to arrive, which calls happened to get returned and which intermediaries happened to remember the mandate.

Summary

Proprietary deal flow is now built, not found. Continuous data capture, thesis-based scoring and governed autonomy compress the cost of reaching a target before the market does. The commercial tools supply the raw material, the scoring model applies the thesis and the human holds the gate. Firms that run the origination layer as infrastructure convert it into a repeatable cost advantage. Firms that rely on the teaser queue buy the residue of everyone else's access.
